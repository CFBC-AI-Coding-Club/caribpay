## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product, and unusually so for a student prototype.** Three things could not be lifted into a generic fintech app without breaking: the Nocturne/daylight split, which carries real semantic load (`ui/Card.tsx:45` gradient card = money you hold; `ui/Screen.tsx:124` gradient header = identity); the settlement timeline (`ui/Timeline.tsx:98`), which turns an architectural fact into the emotional centre of the product; and the hand-drawn circular SVG flags (`components/Flag.tsx:12-100`) with real currency symbols ("EC$", "J$", "Bds$", "TT$"), which locate the app in the Caribbean rather than in generic-currency-land. The fee-free claim is presented as data, not marketing — `review.tsx:248-255` puts "Free" and "No US$" in the same detail table as the rate, which is exactly right.

Where it falls back to category-interchangeable moves: the four-tab bar with a "Menu" tab that re-lists two destinations already in the bar (`menu.tsx:148-159`); Home's 4-tile quick-action row (`home.tsx:33-38`), the single most reproduced neobank pattern in existence; a bell icon that leads to a transaction list; and the Welcome screen's ghosted-verb marquee (`welcome.tsx:26-34`), which is decoration occupying a grid cell and advertises two capabilities the product does not have ("Save", "Convert").

The system itself is well-conceived, not merely followed. The Two-Blue split, Weight-Is-A-Family, and Press-Drops-The-Glow rules each solve a real contrast or React Native problem rather than describing a taste, and all three are honoured with zero violations across ~6,500 lines. The gap between the *system's* quality and the *screens'* execution is where every finding below lives.

**Named Rules — honoured:** Weight-Is-A-Family (zero executable `fontWeight`; the only two occurrences are comment prose explaining the ban, `theme/type.ts:4,7`), Press Drops The Glow (`ui/Button.tsx:122`), Two-Blue (no inversions found), Eleven Floor (no size below 11), No Sharp Corner (no 0-radius surface; `Card.tsx:136` is the correct exception).

**Named Rules — violated:**
- **The Tabular Rule** — `ui/Badge.tsx:53-57`: `Pill`'s label has no `tabular`. Both quote countdowns route through it (`send/index.tsx:361`, `review.tsx:238`), so the one number that changes every second is the one number that reflows every second. Also non-tabular: list timestamps (`ui/ListRow.tsx:50`) and the feed count (`activity.tsx:72`).
- **The Never Colour-Alone Rule** — two breaks, detailed as Priority Issue 4.
- **The 22 Rule** — four competing gutters, as scored above.
- **The Tinted Light Rule** — `wallet/add.tsx:99` puts `shadow.primary` (the blue money glow) on a *selected radio row*, which is neither money-bearing nor the primary action, so it out-glows the real primary button. `ui/Controls.tsx:49` is the app's only raw `shadowColor: "#000"` — should be ink.
- **Off-palette tokens** — `Flag.tsx:124` and `_layout.tsx:26` use `rgba(14,27,46,…)` (#0E1B2E); ink is #1A1340. `transfer/[id].tsx:215` uses #12805C, not `success` #0E6A4C. Plus four undocumented on-dark white opacities.
- **Type ramp** — `welcome.tsx:29` sets `size={58}`, off the nine-size ramp that DESIGN.md declares has "no exceptions".

**Nocturne Rule:** defensible. `welcome.tsx:12` paints the gradient as a full page background, which the rule forbids, but splash and auth are blessed and Welcome is pre-auth identity. Tighten DESIGN.md's wording rather than the code.

`detect.mjs --json apps/mobile` exited 0 with an empty array across 53 scannable files. That is **engine blindness, not token compliance**, and it was worth establishing rather than reporting as a pass:

React Native's camelCase `StyleSheet` objects match none of it. A control test confirmed this: a synthetic CSS/HTML file fired 3 findings; a synthetic `.tsx` file with the same anti-patterns fired 0.
- The design-system colour check is gated by `isProbablyColorLiteral`, which requires the hex to be preceded by a key in a fixed set (`color|background|backgroundColor|borderColor|…`). This codebase's colour keys are `bg:`, `fg:`, `shadowColor:`, and JSX `fill=` — all outside the gate. 18 hexes present in the source were probed; **none** is in the detector's 169-key allowlist, and several (#DCEEF8, #1F5F87, #009739, #CE1126) appear in neither DESIGN.md nor `.impeccable/design.json` — yet all were reported clean.

Practical consequence: if the Impeccable hook is enabled on this project it is giving false comfort on every RN edit. ## Overall Impression

This is a good design system being let down by a handful of screen-level defects, two of which are severe enough to break the demo. The token layer, the honesty of the copy, and the settlement timeline are better than most shipped fintech; the Send composer — the one screen the whole product exists to serve — is where it comes apart, and the amount keypad currently renders in two columns instead of three.

The single biggest opportunity is the Send flow end to end: fix the keypad, format the live amount, make the idempotency key survive a retry, and pin the CTA. That is four contained changes on one screen and one hook, and it moves the product from "impressive but fragile under Q&A" to "solid".

## What's Working

1. **The token layer is load-bearing, not decorative.** `theme/tokens.ts:146` makes `TOUCH_TARGET` platform-aware (44 iOS / 48 Android) instead of shipping Apple's floor to Android hardware. `theme/type.ts:29-40` maps every weight to a registered family because RN will not synthesise weights on Android. `ui/Field.tsx:34-43` draws the focus ring as an absolutely-positioned overlay *and documents why* — border-width growth reflowed the form mid-touch and moved Android focus to the next input. These are decisions with scars on them, not preferences.

2. **Reduce Motion is handled per-animation, with reasoning.** All five `Animated` consumers read `useReducedMotion` — `ui/States.tsx:173` (skeleton holds a steady 0.7), `ui/Timeline.tsx:20` (spinner becomes a static two-tone ring), `transfer/[id].tsx:28` (ripple removed), `index.tsx:15`, `scan.tsx:23` — and each site explains why the reduced state still communicates. All nine `useNativeDriver` flags are `true`. Nothing was globally disabled to tick a box.

3. **Controls that cannot work are absent, and the absence is documented.** `transfer/[id].tsx:337-341` declines to render the design board's "Cancel transfer" button because the architecture has no cancel path. `ui/Controls.tsx:111-119` replaced a hand-rolled switch with the platform `Switch` after concluding the custom one matched neither HIG nor Material 3 for no brand gain. `home.tsx:269-272` changes "See all" to "Add wallet" because Home already lists every wallet. A team refusing to ship lies for visual fidelity is the best signal in this codebase.

## Priority Issues

Six rather than the usual five: two P0s crowded the list, and cutting a P1 that breaks the product's own stated principles would have been dishonest.

### 1. [P0] The numeric keypad renders as two columns, not three

`ui/Keypad.tsx:13-47`. The container is `flexWrap: "wrap"` with `columnGap: space.sm` (8px), and each key is `width: "33.333%"`. Three keys consume 100% of the content box *before* the two 8px gaps are added, so Yoga breaks the line after the second key. The keypad lays out as **2 columns × 6 rows** reading `1 2 / 3 4 / 5 6 / 7 8 / 9 . / 0 ⌫`, with no `rowGap`, at ~288px tall instead of 192px. It also pushes the `Review transfer` button (`send/index.tsx:395`, inside the ScrollView) off-screen.

**Why it matters:** this is the sole input for the app's core task, and the digit order is scrambled. Anyone demoing this will fumble the amount on stage.

**Fix:** percentage widths and pixel gaps cannot coexist here. Drop `columnGap` and give each key `width: "33.3333%"` with `paddingHorizontal: 4` internally, or use three fixed-width keys per row with `justifyContent: "space-between"`. Add `rowGap: 4`. Verify at 320pt width.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### 2. [P0] Retrying a failed transfer can send the money twice — and the copy invites the retry

`api/hooks.ts:220` generates `idempotencyKey: randomId()` inside `mutationFn`. `review.tsx:148-165` then shows "Could not send this transfer — **Nothing has left your wallet**" on *any* error, including a bare fetch rejection where the POST may well have reached the server. The user taps `Confirm & send` again, a new key is minted, and the server sees a fresh request. CLAUDE.md's rule 5 and every server-side idempotency guarantee are defeated at the client. `client.ts` also has no `AbortController`, so the spinner can hang indefinitely first.

**Why it matters:** for the target user on mobile data, sending money that matters, this is the worst possible outcome — and the reassuring copy is the thing that causes it.

**Fix:** mint the key once per draft (store it in `stores/draft.ts` beside the quote, clear it in `reset()`) so a retry replays instead of duplicating. Change the generic-error copy to state only what is known: *"We couldn't confirm this transfer. Check your Transfers list before trying again — we'll show it there if it went through."* Add a 20s `AbortController` timeout to `apiRequest`. Then design the missing third state: a transfer whose outcome is genuinely unknown.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### 3. [P1] The amount the user types is the only unformatted money in the app

`send/index.tsx:284-288` renders `` `${CURRENCY_SYMBOLS[sourceCurrency]}${draft.amount}` `` — the raw keypad string. Typing 25000 shows **`EC$25000`** directly above **`J$1,444,500.00`** (`:334`), and `review.tsx:194` then re-renders the same figure as **`EC$25,000.00`**. The digit-count check that matters most — 2,500 or 25,000? — happens on the one number in the product with no thousands separators and no cents, in a format that changes on the next screen. `formatAmount` and `group()` already exist in `packages/shared/src/currency.ts:80`.

**Why it matters:** an order-of-magnitude error on a remittance is unrecoverable, and this is precisely the screen where the user is counting digits.

**Fix:** run the live figure through `group()` on the integer part while preserving the trailing "." and partial cents as typed (`EC$25,000.` → `EC$25,000.5` → `EC$25,000.50`). Add `numberOfLines={1}` and `adjustsFontSizeToFit` so twelve digits don't wrap the panel.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### 4. [P1] Status is communicated by colour alone in two places — the one rule this product cannot break

DESIGN.md and PRODUCT.md both commit to "status is never colour alone — always an icon and a word".

(a) `ui/Timeline.tsx:150-180` — `TimelineCompact` renders bare 8px dots: green for done, red for failed, and `rgba(26,19,64,0.18)` for **both** active and upcoming. On a pending transfer, "Pending settlement" and "Settled" carry identical grey dots, so the current step is unmarked, and done-vs-failed is colour only. The full `Timeline` gets this right with check and cross glyphs; the compact variant was never held to the same rule.

(b) `home.tsx:255-261` — `weeklyChange` is printed in `color.gainOnDark` (mint) unconditionally, while `useWeeklyChange` (`:114-129`) returns `formatAmount(net, currency, { sign: "always" })` where `net` is frequently negative. The persona's typical week is net negative — sending money out is the whole product — so the home card will read **"−EC$250.00 this week" in the colour DESIGN.md reserves "exclusively for a positive weekly delta"**.

**Why it matters:** this is the product's own honesty principle failing on its two most-viewed surfaces, and it fails hardest for colour-blind and low-vision users.

**Fix:** give `TimelineCompact` the same glyph vocabulary as `Timeline` (check / spinner ring / hollow outline / cross) or drop the variant and reuse `Timeline`. Make the weekly-change colour follow the sign, and pair it with a direction icon plus a word ("out" / "in").

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### 5. [P1] Six XCD countries cannot sign up, because nobody drew their flags

`register.tsx:25` builds the country picker from `FLAG_COUNTRIES` (`packages/shared/src/constants.ts:30`) = `["KN","JM","BB","TT","VC","US"]`. `COUNTRY_NAMES` (`:33-46`) knows six more — Antigua & Barbuda, Anguilla, Dominica, Grenada, St. Lucia, Montserrat — all of them XCD, all squarely the target market, none of them selectable.

**Why it matters:** a missing design asset is silently deciding who is allowed to use a product whose tagline is "One region. One payment." Half the ECCU cannot open an account.

**Fix:** drive the picker from the supported-country list, and give `Flag` a fallback — a currency-tinted disc with the ISO code — for countries without drawn geometry. Then draw the six missing flags.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### 6. [P1] "Scan QR" is a segment that abandons the flow it sits in, and the scanner will not survive a native build

Two independent failures on the same feature.

*Navigation:* `contact/add.tsx:68-74` and `send/index.tsx:187-195` both use a segmented control whose second option pushes `/scan`. On success, `scan.tsx:95-101` sets the *send draft* recipient and `router.replace("/send")` — so a user who taps "Scan QR" **inside Add Contact** lands on the Send composer with no contact saved and no explanation. On Send, because `mode` stays `"scan"` after returning, the screen renders `<Loading label="Opening the scanner…" />` (`:201`) permanently — a spinner for something that will never happen. Segmented controls mean "filter this view" on both platforms; using one for navigation misfires for anyone who reads labels literally.

*Configuration:* `app.json:28` lists only `["expo-router", "expo-secure-store"]`. The `expo-camera` config plugin is absent and there is no `NSCameraUsageDescription`, so scan-to-pay works in Expo Go (which carries its own camera entitlement) and fails outright in any EAS/native build. `app.json:6` also sets `"orientation": "default"` and `"supportsTablet": true` while PRODUCT.md records landscape and tablet as "not yet addressed" — and `transfer/[id].tsx:204` and `receive.tsx:75` are fixed `flex: 1` columns with no `ScrollView`, so rotating during settlement puts the buttons out of reach.

**Fix:** make Send's recipient step two segments (Contact / Address) plus a distinct "Scan a QR code" button, and reset `mode` on focus. Give `/scan` a `returnTo` param so it resolves back into Add Contact's address field. Add the `expo-camera` plugin with a permission string. Set `"orientation": "portrait"` until landscape is designed, and wrap those two screens in a `ScrollView` with `contentContainerStyle={{ flexGrow: 1 }}`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

## Emotional Journey

**Where it reassures, better than most:** the pending wait is the app's best moment — `transfer/[id].tsx:276-294` pairs "Sending to Marcus…" with the amount, "usually a few seconds", a live three-step rail, and a spinner marker, so waiting feels like progress. The failure copy is honest in a way most fintech is not: "Transfer failed / **No money left your wallet.**" plus "Your hold was reversed in full." `useSettlementAnnouncement` (`:101-119`) speaks the outcome once, and only the outcome.

**Where it goes cold, at the exact moment:**
1. **The confirm tap.** `review.tsx:291` swaps the label for a spinner and nothing else changes — no timeout, no "this takes a few seconds", no "don't close the app". The most anxious moment in the product is a 52px spinner on a frozen screen.
2. **The network drop right after.** The app tells the user "Nothing has left your wallet" for a generic fetch rejection — a case where it cannot know. This is the coldest failure in the app, because it is the app confidently saying something false about money.
3. **Rate drift mid-reach.** `review.tsx:168-228` shrinks the avatar 66→60, drops the address, drops the name 20pt→17pt, and inserts a `Notice` plus three table rows — all pushing the primary button down while the thumb is already travelling toward it. DESIGN.md forbids exactly this.
4. **Nobody says you can leave.** During settlement, "Back to home" is a *ghost* button (`transfer/[id].tsx:347`) — the lowest-emphasis treatment in the system — with no copy confirming the transfer continues without you.
5. **The arrival has no echo.** "Done" replaces to Home, where the balance quietly refetches. The peak is on a screen the user is leaving, and the end is silent.

- `send/index.tsx:401`: `Review transfer` shows a spinner while the *quote* loads, conflating "we're pricing this" with "we're submitting this".
- `home.tsx:213-264` labels the figure "Total balance" with no note that non-home currencies were converted at today's rate — a small honesty gap in a product otherwise scrupulous about this.
- `receive.tsx:34-52` sets `copied` for 2s with no live region, so the confirmation is invisible to a screen reader.
- `formatAmount` uses U+2212 MINUS (`currency.ts:118`) — typographically right, but unlikely to be tabular-width in Plus Jakarta Sans, so signed columns will misalign slightly despite the Tabular Rule.
- `keyboardVerticalOffset` is absent on all four `KeyboardAvoidingView` screens; `wallet/add.tsx` has none, correctly, since it has no `TextInput`.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Should the amount composer be the second Nocturne surface?** DESIGN.md's thesis is that you are either looking at what you *have* (nocturne) or *doing* something with it (daylight). But composing an amount is the moment you act *on* a balance — and right now that moment is a white panel with a fake swap button and the app's only unformatted number. What if the "You send / They receive" converter wore the gradient, in the colour that means money?
2. **Why is the keypad an on-screen 12-key grid at all?** It costs the system numeric keyboard's accessibility, haptics, and font scaling; it produced the layout bug; and it forces the CTA below the fold. A `TextInput` with `keyboardType="decimal-pad"` plus a large tabular display gives Sam a real editable value, gives Casey a familiar keyboard, and frees ~190px for a pinned action zone. What is the keypad buying?
3. **The settlement timeline is the best thing in this product, and it lives on a screen the user leaves in five seconds.** What if pending state were visible from Home — the balance card reading "EC$250 in flight to Jamaica" with the rail beneath it — so the product's honesty about money is ambient rather than modal?
4. **What does the UI look like when a transfer's outcome is genuinely unknown?** The app currently resolves that uncertainty by asserting "Nothing has left your wallet." A product whose principle is "say the true thing about money at every moment" needs a designed *unknown* state, as a third status beside pending and failed.
5. **Does "Menu" earn a quarter of the tab bar?** It holds Profile, a link to Add-wallet mislabelled "Wallets", and links to two tabs already in the bar. Move Profile to a Home avatar, and the fourth slot is free — with the strongest candidate being the thing the product is actually about: a **Send** tab, putting the primary task one tap from anywhere instead of two.
